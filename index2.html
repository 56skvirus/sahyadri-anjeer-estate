About Our Farm

Three decades of Anjeer cultivation in the Purandar hills

Sahyadri Anjeer Estate began in 1994 on 4 acres of rocky Purandar soil — the same terroir that gives this region its reputation for the country's finest figs. What started as one family's orchard has grown into a 38-acre estate, without ever losing the habits that built it: hand-selection at harvest, patient soil management, and direct relationships with the people who buy our fruit.

We are farmers first. Every grading decision, every packing choice, is made by people who have walked these rows since childhood — not outsourced to a trading desk. That's the difference companies notice when they work with us for more than one season.

Our Fig Production

From orchard to export-ready crate

Our estate sits at 640m elevation in Purandar taluka, Pune district — a microclimate of well-drained rocky soil and low humidity that Anjeer figs favour. Here is how a fig moves from branch to buyer.

01

Cultivation

Drip-irrigated orchards, pruned twice yearly, with minimal chemical intervention.

02

Harvest

Two seasons — Feb–Apr and Aug–Oct — hand-picked at first light.

03

Fruit sorted by ripeness and visual quality within an hour of picking.

04

Sized and graded to buyer specification using calibrated grading trays.

05

Packing

Ventilated punnets or bulk crates, labelled and palletised for dispatch.

Sustainability

Farming that outlasts a single harvest

Purandar's soil is thin and rocky — it does not forgive shortcuts. Every practice on this estate is chosen to keep the land productive for the next generation, not just the next season.

  • Water Conservation

    Drip irrigation cuts water use by roughly 40% versus flood irrigation.

  • Natural Farming

    Compost and neem-based inputs form the base of our fertility programme.

  • Healthy Soil

    Cover cropping and rotation keep organic matter building year on year.

  • Minimal Chemical Use

    Targeted, low-dose intervention only when pressure testing requires it.

  • Environmental Responsibility

    Native hedge rows maintained along field borders for pollinator habitat.
